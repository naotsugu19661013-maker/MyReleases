# Proposal profile

## Responsibility

- **Purpose:** enable a sponsor or product owner to decide whether, why, and within what business or user boundaries an initiative should proceed.
- **Primary readers:** sponsor, project owner, product owner, affected business stakeholders, and reviewers who authorize further definition.
- **Must decide:** problem or opportunity, target users, intended value, goals and non-goals, initial scope, principal constraints, alternatives at decision level, expected outcomes, major risks, and the next decision gate.
- **Must not decide:** detailed system behavior, complete requirements, class or API design, algorithms, screen-level specifications, or task-by-task implementation instructions.

## Recommended reading order

1. Decision requested and executive summary.
2. Background, problem or opportunity, and affected users.
3. Goals, value, and measurable success indicators.
4. Proposed scope, non-goals, assumptions, and constraints.
5. Conceptual solution and meaningful alternatives.
6. Feasibility, major dependencies, costs or effort range, and risks.
7. Proposed phases and next approval gate.
8. Unresolved decisions, references, and revision history.

## Chapter model

Mandatory subjects are decision requested, background, users, goals, scope/non-goals, conceptual proposal, constraints/risks, and next decision. Conditional subjects include market or stakeholder analysis, alternatives, cost-benefit, legal or policy concerns, and phased rollout.

Separate detailed requirements into a requirements definition, solution structure into design documents, and executable work sequencing into an implementation plan.

## Common failures

- Presenting an attractive feature list without the problem, target users, or decision requested.
- Promising a specific implementation before requirements and constraints are known.
- Mixing rejected brainstorming, meeting comments, and the active proposal.
- Treating projected benefits as confirmed facts without stating assumptions or measures.

## Completion criteria

A decision-maker can explain why the initiative exists, who benefits, what is and is not proposed, the important uncertainty and risk, and exactly what authorization or next step is being requested.
